Stop losing admissions to insurance holds.

By the time your team finishes verifying coverage, the referral has gone cold or gone elsewhere. Here is how to shrink that window with AI, without touching a clinical decision.

3 min readBy Simerson Ai

Here is the pattern that quietly costs you beds: a referral lands at 7 p.m. The patient looks like a good fit, but before anyone can say yes, someone has to verify coverage, confirm benefit days, and read the chart. That work drags into the night, and by the time you have an answer the bed is still empty, or the patient has gone to whoever answered first.

You cannot rush a clinical call, and you should not. But most of what slows an admission down is not the clinical call. It is the paperwork in front of it. That is the part AI is genuinely good at right now, and you can set up a simple version this week.

The 3 step setup

1
Decide what a clean yes needs. Write down the five things that gate an admission at your facility: active coverage, benefit days, authorization path, clinical fit, and any red flags. That short list is the checklist your AI will run every time.
2
Let AI do the eligibility legwork. A tool can pull demographics and payer details off the referral, run the eligibility check, and come back with active coverage, the plan, and the authorization path, so your team starts with an answer instead of a hold.
3
Keep the clinical call human. The AI gathers, verifies, and drafts. Your nurse and your team make the admit decision, every time, and a person approves anything that goes back to the hospital or the family.

An empty bed is not a pause. It is an admission that went to the facility that said yes first.

Keep it compliant

Three rules keep this safe. Never let the AI make a clinical, coverage, or admission decision. Keep a human approving anything patient facing. And make sure any vendor touching patient data signs a BAA. That is the line between a helpful assistant and a liability.

That is it. A short checklist, the eligibility work done before your team wakes up, and the clinical call left where it belongs, with your people. Most admissions teams that do this stop deciding at midnight and start deciding by lunch.
